Born-Digital

Introduction

In her single-channel video Vessel, Bianca Shonee Arroyo-Kreimes envisions a “carrier” whose bright, womb-like pod nurtures early life. The carrier is both organic and synthetic, evoking a ship as well as a mermaid’s purse. It floats through a world where every surface shimmers, signaling the gorgeous uncertainty of its hybrid form.

I am struck by the carrier’s mode of travel. Despite its synthetic exterior and perfect, machinelike symmetry, the carrier’s movement isn’t strictly procedural: it drifts. Arroyo-Kreimes is interested in the panspermic transfer of consciousness across the universe. Alongside technology’s sanctioned modes of replication — the transference, duplication, and compilation required to publish Vessel — she frames technology through germination, scatter, and the steady drift of seed pods in water.

Drunken Boat’s “Born-Digital” folio explores this drift and the carriers that enact it. Emily d’Achiardi’s sketches carry the textual residue of various images: a cat, a pile of stones, a view from Antelope Island State Park. Jenna Caravello’s Amber Row explores grief through a video game in which the player labors to gather and carry memories as collectible objects. Sarah Rothberg’s performance Human in the Loop uses audience members’ conversation to train a generative AI system. This data is reconstituted and performed as a script, allowing actors to carry the traces, interpretations, and distortions of a text that preceded them.

The act of carrying demonstrates the inextricability of memory from the systems that process it. How does technology compress, render, and propagate human sentiment? How do digital systems, as carriers, shape and preserve their content? How do we?

This collection of incredible, singular works invites you to consider carrying as a conscious and unconscious process. It can be an act of attachment, burden, or support. It can be passive too, as one carries residue, and it can be both at once. If carrying implies an object — we carry something or someone — then these works ask us to consider what we carry and how. They transport us through these questions without fixity or destination, like a Vessel slowly drifting through new lands.
